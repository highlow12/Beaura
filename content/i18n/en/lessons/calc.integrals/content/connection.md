# Interpreting Accumulation and Checking Units

Integration adds small contributions to form a total, so identify what is being accumulated with respect to what, and track the units. Integrating a quantity already given per unit time, such as velocity, with respect to time gives displacement. Where a function is negative, directional quantities can cancel.

For example, if velocity is `v(t)=2t m/s` for `0≤t≤3`, the displacement is `∫₀³2t dt=[t²]₀³=9 m`. If the interval includes regions where velocity is negative, this integral gives signed displacement. To find the total distance traveled, integrate the absolute value of velocity or split the interval where its sign changes.

So do not automatically call an integral area or distance. Check what results from multiplying the integrand’s units by the integration variable’s units, and whether negative contributions should cancel.
