# Predicting a long-run average

The expected value of a discrete random variable X is the probability-weighted sum of its possible values: E[X]=Σx·P(X=x). It can be interpreted as the long-run average over many repetitions of the same experiment, not as the result of one trial.

Expected value is linear: E[aX+b]=aE[X]+b and E[X+Y]=E[X]+E[Y]. The expected value of a sum is the sum of the expected values, whether or not X and Y are independent.
