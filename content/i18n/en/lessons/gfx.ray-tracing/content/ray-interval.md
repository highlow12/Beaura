## Ray Parameters and Valid Intersections

When a ray is written as `P(t) = O + tD`, `t` is a parameter that identifies a position along the ray. If the direction vector `D` is normalized to unit length, `t` equals the actual distance traveled from the origin. Under this condition, if a ray intersects a shape at `t = 2` and `t = 5`, the camera ray sees the nearer surface at `t = 2`. A solution with `t < 0` lies behind the ray's origin and must be discarded when tracing a ray (a half-line), rather than an infinite line. For a shadow ray, if the light is 3 units away but a blocker intersects at `t = 5`, that object is behind the light and does not block it. If `D` is not normalized, do not compare `t` directly with a distance; convert both to the same parameter interval.

If a reflected ray starts exactly at the intersection point on the same surface, floating-point error can make it hit the surface again and cause acne. A small epsilon offset along the normal prevents this. If the epsilon is too large, nearby real objects may be skipped, so tune it to the scene's scale. A BVH does not remove all triangles; it reduces the candidates that might intersect a ray. Even with fewer candidates, you still need to choose the smallest valid `t` at the end.
