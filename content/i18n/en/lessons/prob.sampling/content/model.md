# Interpret a Sample Alongside Its Sampling Design

Whether a sample is representative depends not only on its size but also on who had a chance to be selected. Random sampling reduces selection bias within a defined population, but it cannot fix a sampling frame that excluded some people from the start.

Suppose you want to measure satisfaction among all app users but survey only those online from 8 to 9 p.m. Users who are more likely to be active then may be overrepresented. Randomly selecting 100 people from the full member list and recording response rates and missing groups provides information closer to the target population.

A common mistake is to assume that a larger biased sample becomes representative. Repeatedly sampling the same group may reduce random variation in the sample mean, but systematic omissions remain. Check sample size and selection bias separately.
