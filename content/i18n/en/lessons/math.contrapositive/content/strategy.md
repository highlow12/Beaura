# When to Choose the Contrapositive

A contrapositive proof is especially useful when the original conclusion is difficult to prove directly, but starting with its negation simplifies the argument. Since the contrapositive has the same truth value as the original statement, proving the contrapositive is sufficient.

For example, the contrapositive of “if `n²` is divisible by 4, then `n` is even” is “if `n` is odd, then `n²` is not divisible by 4.” Write `n = 2k + 1`; then `n² = 4k(k + 1) + 1`, so its remainder is clearly 1.

A common mistake is to negate only the consequent or only reverse the order, producing the converse. Write negations of compound conditions precisely, such as “not divisible by 4,” and confirm at the end that the statement you proved is the original statement's contrapositive.
