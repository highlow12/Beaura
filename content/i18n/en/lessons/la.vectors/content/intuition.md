# Visualizing Vectors as Joined Arrows

Vector addition is easier when vectors are thought of as displacement arrows rather than points attached to a location. If `u = (2, 1)` and `v = (−1, 2)`, first move by `u`, then by `v` from its endpoint; the total displacement is `u + v = (1, 3)`. Translating the same two arrows to form a parallelogram gives the same diagonal.

Scalar multiplication changes an arrow's length and direction. `2u` doubles its length in the same direction, `−u` keeps its length but points the opposite way, and `0u` is a point of zero length. The vector from point `P` to `Q` is `Q − P`, so do not confuse the position `P` with the displacement `Q − P`.

When adding components, pair x with x and y with y. The sum of lengths `|u| + |v|` is generally not the length of `u + v`, since directions can cancel each other. After calculating, check whether the arrow's approximate direction and signs make sense.
