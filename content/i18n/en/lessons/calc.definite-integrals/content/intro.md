# Accumulation over an Interval

The definite integral ∫ₐᵇ f(x)dx is the signed accumulation of a function from a to b. By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, find an antiderivative F with F′=f, then calculate ∫ₐᵇf=F(b)−F(a).

When a function is above the x-axis, its definite integral equals the area between the graph and the x-axis. Regions below the axis accumulate as negative values, so split the interval or use an absolute value to find total geometric area.
