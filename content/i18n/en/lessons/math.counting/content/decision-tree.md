# Establishing a Way to Split Cases

When counting outcomes, first divide them into non-overlapping cases and make sure every outcome belongs to one case. Multiply the choices when several steps are all performed within a case, and add the outcomes of separate cases.

For example, suppose there are 3 menu items that each come with 2 side-dish choices, and 2 items with no side dish. There are `3×2 + 2 = 8` outcomes in total. The two terms do not overlap because the cases with and without a side dish were separated first.

Common mistakes include always multiplying cases joined by “or,” or using one product when the number of choices differs by step. Drawing a small decision tree and checking what each branch selects can reduce double counting and omissions.
