# Factorials and Filling Positions

If choosing a chair and vice-chair from 5 people, there are 5 choices for the first position and 4 for the second, so 5P2=20. Since the roles differ, swapping the choices counts as a different outcome.

Permutation problems can also be solved by drawing the positions first and writing the number of choices for each. The basic permutation formula does not apply unchanged when repetition is allowed or some objects are identical.
