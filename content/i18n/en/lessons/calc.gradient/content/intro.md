# The Direction of Steepest Increase

The gradient ∇f of a scalar function f(x,y) is a vector whose components are the partial derivatives with respect to each variable. It is ∇f=(∂f/∂x,∂f/∂y), and at a point where ∇f is not 0, it points in the direction of the function’s greatest increase. At a point where ∇f=0, the first-order approximation cannot determine an increasing direction.

The directional derivative in the direction of vector u is ∇f·u; when u is a unit vector, this gives the instantaneous rate of change in that direction. To first order, the function value does not change in a direction perpendicular to the gradient.
