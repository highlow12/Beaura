# Checking for independence

The outcomes of two coin tosses are independent because the first toss does not change the probabilities for the second. By contrast, if you draw a card and do not replace it before drawing the next, the first draw changes the remaining deck, so the draws are usually dependent.

To claim that events in data are independent, check conditional probabilities or the product rule for their intersection. Do not conclude that events are independent just because they are different.
