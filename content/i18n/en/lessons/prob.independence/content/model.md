# Independence depends on how outcomes are generated

To determine independence, check whether observing one event changes the probability of the other. Events with the same names can be independent or dependent depending on how the draws or trials are carried out.

Suppose we draw twice from a bag with 2 red and 3 blue balls. If we replace the first ball, let `A` mean the first ball is red and `B` the second is red. Then `P(A)=P(B)=2/5` and `P(A∩B)=4/25`, so the events are independent. Without replacement, `P(B|A)=1/4`, so they are not independent.

A common misconception is to think that different events, or mutually exclusive events, are independent. Independence is not about event names; it means that information about one event does not change the probability of another. Mutually exclusive events with positive probability cannot be independent because they cannot occur together.
