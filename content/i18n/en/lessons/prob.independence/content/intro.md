# When information does not change a probability

Events A and B are independent when P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B). This product rule expresses independence even when one event has probability 0, where conditional probability is not defined.

When P(B)>0, independence is equivalent to P(A|B)=P(A). In other words, knowing that B occurred does not change the probability of A. Likewise, if P(A)>0, then P(B|A)=P(B).

Independence is different from mutual exclusivity. If two mutually exclusive events both have nonzero probability, they cannot occur together and therefore cannot be independent.
