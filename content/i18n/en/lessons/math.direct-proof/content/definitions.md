# Turning Definitions into Equations

The key to a direct proof is to choose an arbitrary object, turn the definitions in the statement into equations, and check that the final expression fits the definition of the goal. It is important to state which integers the symbols represent, not just to get the calculation right.

For example, if `a` and `b` are multiples of 3, then `a = 3m` and `b = 3n` for some integers `m` and `n`. Thus, `a + b = 3m + 3n = 3(m + n)`. Since `m + n` is also an integer, the sum is a multiple of 3.

Common mistakes include substituting a few numbers and claiming a general statement, or omitting the condition that `m` and `n` are integers. Check that you began with an “arbitrary” object and that the final result exactly satisfies the goal's definition.
