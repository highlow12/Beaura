# The Instantaneous Rate of Change

The average rate of change is the change in y divided by the change in x between two points. Letting the gap between the points approach 0 gives the instantaneous rate of change at one point. The value f′(a) at a particular point a is called the derivative at that point, and the function f′(x) of these values is called the derivative.

f′(a) is the slope of the tangent to the graph y=f(x) at x=a. Derivatives measure how a quantity changes at each step: the derivative of position is velocity, and the derivative of velocity is acceleration.
