# Base rates and all the evidence

When evaluating a rare event, as in disease testing, distinguish the chance of disease given a positive result, P(disease|positive), from the chance of a positive result given disease, P(positive|disease). The first is not simply the inverse of the second.

When there are multiple possible causes, use the law of total probability to find P(B). If the prior P(A) is low, the posterior may be lower than intuition suggests even when the likelihood is high. Count each path in a table or tree diagram.
