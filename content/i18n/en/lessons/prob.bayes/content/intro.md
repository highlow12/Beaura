# Inferring causes from outcomes

Bayes' theorem states that P(A|B)=P(B|A)P(A)/P(B) when P(B)>0. It provides a way to work backward from observed evidence B to the probability of its cause A.

P(A) is called the prior, P(B|A) the likelihood, and P(A|B) the posterior. P(B) is the total probability of observing the evidence across all possible causes; omitting this denominator can make the posterior exceed 1 or become much too large.
