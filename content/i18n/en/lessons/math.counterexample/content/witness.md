# A Counterexample Is a Witness That Meets the Conditions

The negation of the universal statement `for every x, P(x)` is `there exists an x for which P(x) is false`. So, when looking for a counterexample, do not choose an arbitrary value; find a witness that meets the original conditions while disproving the conclusion.

For example, the claim “every integer greater than 1 is prime” is disproved by `4`. It meets the condition of being greater than 1, but it has divisors other than 1 and itself, so it is not prime.

A common mistake is to offer a value that does not satisfy the conditions. Also, one counterexample shows only that a universal statement is false; it does not mean all values are counterexamples or prove the universal statement in the opposite direction.
